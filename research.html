Research

Predicting UK Patent-Validity Court Outcomes

Paper (PDF)

Abstract. Predicting litigation outcomes often requires combining information from documents that serve different roles and cannot always be interpreted independently. I examine three questions: which documents provide useful predictive information, whether documents should be encoded separately or jointly, and how to represent text whose meaning depends on other text. To investigate these questions, I construct a new dataset of U.K. patent litigation outcomes from 1995 to 2026 and compare predictive models at the patent, claim, and ground-of-invalidity levels. Patent litigation provides a setting in which these choices matter: assessing validity may require comparing a claim with the patent description or earlier technical disclosures, while dependent claims incorporate limitations from other claims. I define maximum dependency depth to measure the longest chain of these dependencies and construct expanded claim representations that explicitly incorporate inherited limitations. The results suggest that encoding documents separately and then combining their representations can outperform jointly encoding the concatenated texts in this setting.
